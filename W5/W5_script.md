## Situation

You have completed John's subjective assessment and physical examination. Based on his symptoms, medical history and assessment findings, you have developed an initial clinical impression.

You now want to explain what you think might be contributing to his lower back pain, using clear and patient-friendly language.

## Dialogue

Physio: Alright, John. We've covered quite a bit today. I'd like to talk through what I think might be going on with your back. Is that okay?

Patient: Yeah, sure.

Physio: So, based on what you've told me and what I've found during the assessment, it seems that bending forward is one of the movements that's currently aggravating your back pain.

Patient: Yeah, definitely. That's when I notice it the most.

Physio: Right. What I'm thinking is that your back is currently quite sensitive to certain movements, particularly bending forward. There could be a few reasons for this, so I wouldn't want to jump to any conclusions just yet.

Patient: What do you mean by sensitive? Does that mean I've damaged something in my back?

Physio: Not necessarily. Pain doesn't always mean there's ongoing damage. Sometimes, the structures around your back can become more sensitive, and certain movements can trigger your symptoms more easily.

Patient: Okay. So, what do you think is actually causing it?

Physio: At this stage, my impression is that your pain is being aggravated by certain movements rather than one specific activity or injury. I'd like to consider everything we've discussed today before drawing any firm conclusions.

Patient: I see. Is it something I should be worried about?

Physio: From what we've discussed and found today, I haven't identified anything that immediately suggests a serious problem. We'll continue to monitor your symptoms and how you respond to treatment.

Patient: Okay, that makes me feel a bit better.

Physio: Good. Does that sound similar to what you've been experiencing?

Patient: Yeah, that makes sense. I just want to understand what's happening and how I can get back to normal.

Physio: Absolutely. That's what we'll work towards. The good thing is that we've identified some movements that are currently more challenging for you. We can use that information to guide your treatment and gradually build your confidence with movement again.

Patient: Sounds good.

Physio: Do you have any questions about what we've discussed so far?

Patient: No, I think that makes sense. Thanks for explaining.

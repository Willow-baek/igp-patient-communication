## Situation

You have completed John's subjective assessment and physical examination. Based on his symptoms, medical history and assessment findings, you have developed an initial clinical impression.

You now want to explain what you think might be contributing to his lower back pain, using clear and patient-friendly language.

## Dialogue

Physio: Alright, John. We've covered quite a bit today. I'd like to explain what I think might be going on with your back. Is that okay?

John: Yeah, sure.

Physio: Based on what you've told me and what I've found during the assessment, it seems that your back has become quite sensitive to certain movements and activities.

John: Yeah, even simple things like putting my shoes on can be uncomfortable.

Physio: I understand. Sometimes, when you've had pain for a while, your body can become a bit more protective. It's almost like a smoke alarm. Its job is to warn you about danger, but sometimes it can go off even when there's no real danger.

John: So, does that mean I've damaged something in my back?

Physio: Not necessarily. Pain doesn't always mean something is damaged. Sometimes, your body is just being extra careful and sending out pain signals more easily than usual.

John: I see. So, is it something I should be worried about?

Physio: From what we've discussed and found today, I haven't identified anything that immediately suggests a serious problem. We'll keep an eye on your symptoms as we go.

John: Okay, that's good to hear. So, what can we do about it?

Physio: We can start by finding movements that feel comfortable for you and gradually build things up. The aim is to help your back feel more comfortable with movement again.

John: That sounds good. I just want to get back to normal.

Physio: Absolutely. We'll work towards that together, one step at a time.

John: Thanks. That makes a bit more sense now.

Physio: No worries. Do you have any other questions about what we've discussed?

John: Not for now! Thanks.
